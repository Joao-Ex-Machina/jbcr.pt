## On AI technologically

As of the writing of this post (summer 2026), I still think there is **nothing** that is inherently **ground-breaking** in current AI.

For those not familiar, the architecture of AI is mostly based on matrix multiplications (MatMul) and activation functions, so basic algebra.

Of course you can argue that the way we organize such MatMuls is the true science behind AI, or even the selection of specific activation functions, however I would've to disagree...Most guys in AI, just grid-search or throw clay to wall (or sh*t to the fan) to tune and develop new models...And then "we obtained a 0.01% in this dataset, so I guess it's time to publish a paper"! 
Disregarding power consumption, arithmetic complexity and all those features that are truly relevant. 

The true ground-breaking science on AI, for me, is the **Backpropagation** algorithm, which enables the model to "learn" (in a layman's sense) and update its matrices. The idea for this algorithm exists since the XVII century, but has been **roughly frozen since the 80's**.

But if this algorithm is so ancient, why only recently has AI blasted off?

**The driving force in AI has never been software, it was always the hardware.**
